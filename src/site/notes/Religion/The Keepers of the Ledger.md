The **Keepers of the Ledger** are the dominant religious institution of [[Locations/Primary Locations/Suerré\|Suerré]], overseeing matters of morality, contracts, justice, remembrance, and social obligation. To the faithful, the universe itself is a vast accounting book of records maintained by divine powers known as the **Great Auditors**.

Every action creates a debt or credit. Kindness, honesty, sacrifice, and duty add to one's standing. Cruelty, theft, betrayal, and murder incur debts that must eventually be repaid. Followers believe that no wrongdoing truly goes unrecorded. Even crimes hidden from mortal eyes are entered into the **Eternal Ledger**, the sacred account of all souls.

>[!Quote |mark] Core Tenet
>"The books always balance."

### Theology

#### The Eternal Ledger

The Eternal Ledger is the central theological concept of the Keepers of the Ledger faith. Followers believe it is a metaphysical record containing every action, decision, promise, betrayal, and sacrifice ever made by any living being. According to doctrine, the Ledger exists beyond the material world and is maintained by the Great Auditors, who ensure that no deed, whether virtuous or wicked, is ever forgotten.

Unlike mortal records, the Eternal Ledger cannot be altered, forged, or destroyed. Church doctrine states that while memories may fade and physical evidence may be lost, the Ledger preserves a perfect account of reality. This belief forms the basis for the faith's emphasis on documentation, record-keeping, and investigation.

The Eternal Ledger is often symbolized by a closed black book bound with silver clasps. Artistic depictions commonly portray its pages as endless, with each page capable of containing the complete history of a single life. In religious art, the Great Auditors are frequently shown inscribing new entries into the Ledger with silver quills fashioned from starlight.

The central doctrine holds that reality itself is a living ledger. Every person possesses an invisible account known as a **Soul Balance**.

Nothing can be erased. Debts may be balanced, but never removed.

Actions are categorized as;

| Category     | Effect                                           |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Credits      | Charity, honesty, duty, sacrifice                |
| Debts        | Theft, murder, deception, betrayal               |
| Obligations  | Promise, oaths, contracts                        |
| Inheritances | Debts and duties passed through family or office |

#### The Great Auditors

Rather than worshiping a single god, the faith reveres five divine entities;

| Auditor             | Symbol        | Purpose                                     |
| ------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Auditor of Truth    | Open eye      | Records all lies and hidden knowledge.      |
| Auditor of Duty     | Scales        | Records obligations and responsibilities.   |
| Auditor of Mercy    | Open hand     | Records forgiveness and acts of compassion. |
| Auditor of Judgment | Iron quill    | Records crimes and punishments.             |
| Auditor of Balance  | Closed ledger | Maintains equilibrium among all accounts.   |
>[!Quote |mark] Auditor of Mercy
>"Forgiveness is not forgetting. Forgiveness is accounting."

#### Creation Myth

According to scripture:

*Before existence there was only Chaos, where actions vanished without consequence.*

*The Great Auditors forged the First Ledger and began recording every event.*

*As the Ledger grew, reality gained permanence.*

Thus:

>[!Quote |mark] Core Tenet
>"The world exists because it is recorded."

#### The Afterlife

According to the doctrine of the **Keepers of the Ledger**, death represents the conclusion of a mortal account and the beginning of the **Final Audit**, a metaphysical evaluation in which every action, intention, promise, and consequence accumulated throughout a person's life is reviewed. The faithful believe that upon death, the soul is brought before the **Great Auditors**, who examine the individual's **Soul Balance** as recorded within the **Eternal Ledger**.

Unlike mortal judgement, the Final Audit is believed to consider the entirety of a person's existence rather than isolated events. A single act is not viewed independently, but instead weighed against the circumstances surrounding it, the motivations behind it, and the consequences that followed. The faith teaches that no life can be reduced to a simple measure of good or evil, as every individual carries both credits and debts within their account.

The Keepers reject the idea that death grants freedom from responsibility. According to their teachings, physical existence is only one chapter of a greater accounting process, and actions performed during life continue to influence the state of the soul after death. The Eternal Ledger preserves all records, including forgotten deeds, hidden crimes, unfulfilled promises, and sacrifices unknown to others.

The Final Audit results in one of three possible outcomes.

- **Balanced Souls**

Those whose accounts are considered settled are granted entry into the **Grand Archive**, the afterlife of the Keepers of the Ledger. The Grand Archive is described as an eternal sanctuary of knowledge, where balanced souls exist alongside the records of all things that have ever occurred.

Followers believe that souls accepted into the Grand Archive do not simply rest, but continue the work of preservation. Some are said to become eternal record-keepers, assisting the Great Auditors in maintaining the vast history of existence. In this interpretation, a righteous life does not end with reward, but with continued purpose.

The Grand Archive is often portrayed in religious artwork as an endless library beyond the physical world, containing infinite shelves of books, scrolls, and ledgers. The faithful describe it as a place without suffering or uncertainty, where all questions can eventually be answered because all knowledge is preserved.

- **Indebted Souls**

Those who die carrying significant unresolved debts are believed to undergo **Reconciliation**, a process through which the soul is given the opportunity to restore balance. Rather than being punished eternally, these souls are returned to existence through reincarnation.

According to doctrine, reincarnation is not random, but carefully shaped according to the debts and obligations recorded within the soul's account. A person who abused power may return without privilege. A person who caused suffering may experience the consequences of their actions from another perspective. The purpose of reincarnation is not vengeance, but understanding and correction.

The Keepers teach that a soul cannot simply escape its responsibilities through death. Debts may be repaid, but they cannot be ignored. Each new life represents another opportunity to restore balance and bring the account closer to completion.

- **Irredeemable Souls**

The most severe fate is reserved for those considered **Irredeemable Souls**. These are individuals who, according to doctrine, have deliberately rejected balance itself and committed acts that cannot be corrected through ordinary reconciliation.

Such souls are condemned to the **Blank Pages**, a state of existence defined not by physical suffering, but by complete separation from memory and record. The soul is not destroyed, but removed from the meaningful history of the universe. No records preserve their existence, no stories remember their name, and no future generation carries knowledge of them.

Among followers of the Keepers, this fate is considered the ultimate punishment. Death is accepted as part of the natural order, and suffering is believed to serve a purpose, but being forgotten represents the complete failure of an individual's account. The Blank Pages are therefore viewed as the opposite of the Grand Archive: one preserves all that was, while the other contains what has been abandoned.

#### Morality

- **The Question of Balance**

A central debate within the **Keepers of the Ledger** concerns the relationship between **balance and fairness**. While the faith teaches that every action creates a corresponding entry within the **Eternal Ledger**, scholars and followers have long questioned whether a perfectly balanced account is necessarily a just one.

The Ledger does not measure morality in simple terms of good and evil. Instead, it records actions, consequences, obligations, and sacrifices. A deed may create both debt and credit simultaneously, with the final balance determined by the complete context of an individual's choices.

The traditional example used in theological discussion is the case of the desperate parent.

_A man steals medicine from a wealthy merchant to save his dying child._

The Ledger therefore records:

- **Theft** → Debt
- **Preserving a life** → Credit

The account may ultimately balance. The man's crime is acknowledged, but so is the life preserved through his actions.

However, the question remains: **The Ledger is balanced, but is the weight fair?**

- **The Problem of Perfect Justice**

Philosophers within the Keepers have long debated whether true justice can exist when every action is weighed against another.

If a person commits a terrible act but later saves many lives, does the Ledger consider them redeemed?
If a person commits a crime to prevent a greater tragedy, is the debt still deserved?
If a ruler causes suffering but creates lasting peace, does the Ledger record a tyrant or a saviour?

These questions are not considered flaws within the faith. Instead, they are regarded as evidence of the Ledger's complexity.

The Great Auditors are not believed to ask:

_"Was this person good?"_

They ask:

_"What did this person change?"_

### Worship

#### Daily Reconciliation

Each evening believers record:

- Good deeds performed
- Debts incurred
- Promises made
- Obligations fulfilled

This practice is called **Closing the Day's Account**.

#### Confession

Confession is known as **Disclosure**.

The purpose is not forgiveness. It is correction. A priest determines how the debt should be repaid.

Examples of a priest's determination;

- Stolen money must be returned.
- A lie must be corrected.
- Harm must be compensated.

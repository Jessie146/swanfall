**A Song for the Guilty** begins when a nobleman is murdered inside the [[Locations/Secondary Locations/Suereé Locations/Théâtre de Lune\|Théâtre de Lune]]. With the theatre sealed and suspicion falling on the party, they must investigate the crime before [[Characters/Suerre Characters/Captain Lucien Moreau\|Captain Lucien Moreau]] and the [[Creatures/Humans/Suerré Constable\|Suerré Constables]] decide who is guilty.

>[!Quote |mark] Frightened Theatre Patron
>"In Suerré, innocence isn't decided by the courts... it's decided by whoever reaches the judge first."

#### Description

The music has stopped, but panic continues to echo through the gilded halls of the [[Locations/Secondary Locations/Suereé Locations/Théâtre de Lune\|Théâtre de Lune]]. Moments after the final curtain falls, one of [[Locations/Primary Locations/Suerré\|Suerré]]'s most influential noblemen is discovered murdered within his private balcony, a silver-handled dagger driven cleanly beneath his ribs. Before anyone can flee, heavy iron gates seal every entrance to the theatre.

No guest is permitted to leave.

Rumours spread faster than the guards can contain them. Witnesses contradict one another. Nobles loudly accuse political rivals while servants quietly vanish into the labyrinth of backstage corridors. Amidst the confusion, several attendants identify the party as outsiders seen meeting privately with [[Characters/Suerre Characters/The Nightingale\|The Nightingale]] only moments before the murder.

[[Characters/Suerre Characters/Captain Lucien Moreau\|Captain Lucien Moreau]], commander of the responding constables, wastes little time. To him the circumstances appear simple. Foreign adventurers arrive carrying a mysterious invitation, disappear backstage with the city's most elusive performer, and moments later a prominent noble lies dead. Whether true or not, the story is convenient enough to satisfy the city's anxious elite.

#### Expanded Objectives

 #### 1) Escape the Locked Theatre

The music has stopped, but panic continues to echo through the gilded halls of the [[Locations/Secondary Locations/Suereé Locations/Théâtre de Lune\|Théâtre de Lune]]. Moments after the final curtain falls, one of [[Locations/Primary Locations/Suerré\|Suerré]]’s most influential noblemen is discovered murdered in his private balcony. A silver-handled dagger has been driven beneath his ribs. Before anyone can leave, heavy iron gates seal every entrance to the theatre.

> **Suerré Constable:** "Nobody leaves until we know who did this."

Rumours spread through the theatre. Nobles accuse political rivals, servants disappear into backstage corridors, and witnesses begin changing their stories. Several attendants identify the party as outsiders who were seen speaking privately with [[Characters/Suerre Characters/The Nightingale\|The Nightingale]] shortly before the murder.

[[Characters/Suerre Characters/Captain Lucien Moreau\|Captain Lucien Moreau]], commander of the responding constables, quickly forms his own conclusion. Foreign adventurers arrive carrying a mysterious invitation, meet privately with the city’s most elusive performer, and are present moments before a prominent noble is killed. The explanation is convenient enough for the city’s anxious elite.

> **Captain Lucien Moreau:** "You arrived carrying an invitation no one can explain. Then a man dies, and you're the last people seen with the Nightingale. You understand how that looks."

The party must reach the murder scene before the guards separate them from the investigation.

Possible approaches include:

- Move through the theatre’s public halls while avoiding the constables.
- Follow [[Characters/Suerre Characters/The Nightingale\|The Nightingale]] through hidden backstage passages.
- Escape through the scenery lofts and return to the theatre from above.
- Discover a concealed route beneath the stage.

> **[CUT OF YOUR JIB]** "The guards are watching the doors. The backstage passages are almost empty." **(DC 12)**

On a successful check, the party notices that the constables are concentrating on the main entrances and slips through the backstage corridors.

> **[CERTAIN JE NE SAIS QUOI]** "The theatre was built around older passages. There must be another way through." **(DC 13)**

On a successful check, the party discovers a concealed maintenance passage beneath the stage.

A failed check does not prevent progress, but the party is stopped by a constable and must either persuade them, retreat, or find another route.

Optional discoveries include:

- Hidden smuggling passages beneath the orchestra pit.
- Backstage doors that can only be opened from the inside.
- Signs that someone has recently used the theatre’s older passages.

#### 2) Examine the Crime Scene

The murdered nobleman lies across a velvet chair in his private balcony. The dagger remains beneath his ribs, but several details suggest that the scene is not as straightforward as it first appears. The party may search the balcony before the constables take control of it.

> **[CUT OF YOUR JIB]** "The blood is wrong. He didn't fall where he was stabbed." **(DC 13)**

On a successful check, the party notices blood beneath the chair and determines that the victim was moved after death.

> The blood beneath the chair suggests that the nobleman collapsed somewhere else before being placed in the balcony.

> **[CERTAIN JE NE SAIS QUOI]** "The dagger is meant to be found. Someone wanted us to look at it." **(DC 14)**

On a successful check, the party notices that the weapon bears no fingerprints and appears to have been deliberately positioned.

> **[CHILDLIKE WONDER]** "Why would an assassin leave the weapon behind?" **(DC 10)**

On a successful check, the party discovers traces of unusual black candle wax and an unfamiliar perfume near the balcony entrance.

The party may also discover:

- The dagger was planted after the victim died.
- The balcony door was opened from the inside.
- The victim had been carrying a torn piece of paper.
- Someone searched the nobleman’s coat shortly before the party arrived.

A failed check reveals no useful evidence, but the party can continue by questioning the witnesses.

#### 3) Question the Witnesses

The theatre remains under lockdown. Performers, attendants, musicians, and nobles are trapped inside while the constables attempt to establish what happened. The party may question witnesses before they are separated or escorted away.

> **[MOXIE]** "You saw something. Tell us what happened before the guards decide what you're allowed to remember." **(DC 12)**

On a successful check, a frightened theatre patron admits that several witnesses are deliberately withholding information.

> **Theatre Patron:** "Everyone saw something. The trouble is, everyone saw something different."

> **[CERTAIN JE NE SAIS QUOI]** "Your story sounds rehearsed. Who told you what to say?" **(DC 14)**

On a successful check, the party identifies that some witnesses have been given the same account of the murder.

> **[CHILDLIKE WONDER]** "What did you hear above the stage during the performance?" **(DC 10)**

On a successful check, a musician recalls hearing footsteps above the rafters while the Nightingale was singing.

The party may question:

- [[Characters/Suerre Characters/Madame Celeste\|Madame Celeste]], who knew the murdered nobleman.
- [[Characters/Suerre Characters/Stagehand Émile\|Stagehand Émile]], who worked near the upper balconies.
- A frightened theatre patron.
- The musicians who performed during the evening.
- Nobles who were seated near the victim.

**Madame Celeste**

Madame Celeste admits that the murdered nobleman had received anonymous threats before the performance.

> **Madame Celeste:** "He told me someone wanted him frightened. I thought it was another political rival trying to make a point."

She refuses to say who sent the threats unless the party convinces her that they are investigating the murder rather than trying to escape blame.

**Stagehand Émile**

A musician’s testimony suggests that [[Characters/Suerre Characters/Stagehand Émile\|Stagehand Émile]] may have seen someone moving through the rafters. However, Émile is nowhere to be found. The party must locate him before the constables or the killer can reach him.

#### 4) Locate the Missing Stagehand

The search leads the party through the theatre’s workshops, storage rooms, and scenery lofts. Evidence suggests that Émile did not simply disappear. Someone frightened him into hiding.

The party may discover:

- A dropped stage tool near the rafters.
- Blood on a backstage curtain.
- A broken lantern.
- Footprints leading towards the scenery loft.
- A door that has been forced open from the inside.

> **[CUT OF YOUR JIB]** "Someone left in a hurry. They didn't have time to clean up." **(DC 12)**

On a successful check, the party follows the trail to Émile’s hiding place.

> **[MOXIE]** "We're not here to arrest you. We're here because someone is trying to make sure you never speak." **(DC 13)**

On a successful check, Émile agrees to explain what he saw. Émile reveals that he heard footsteps above the stage during the performance. He later saw a masked figure searching the murdered nobleman’s balcony. Before he can provide a complete description, a [[Creatures/Humans/Masked Assassin\|Masked Assassin]] attacks.

**Optional Encounter: [[Creatures/Humans/Masked Assassin\|Masked Assassin]]**

The party must defeat the assassin before they can silence Émile. If the assassin escapes, Émile survives but is too frightened to provide further information. If the party defeats the assassin, they recover a small piece of black fabric bearing the same perfume found at the crime scene.

#### 5) Recover the Bell Medallion

The party returns to the murdered nobleman’s belongings and searches his coat. Inside, they discover a fractured brass medallion engraved with a bell wrapped in thorned vines, the same symbol embossed upon [[Characters/Silverleaf Woods Characters/Jacque D'aniels\|Jacque D'aniels]]’ invitation. The medallion appears to have been broken deliberately, as though someone attempted to remove part of the design.

> **[CERTAIN JE NE SAIS QUOI]** "The invitation wasn't meant to bring us to the theatre. It was meant to bring this here." **(DC 14)**

On a successful check, the party realises that the invitation and the medallion are connected.

The party presents the medallion to [[Characters/Suerre Characters/The Nightingale\|The Nightingale]]. When she sees it, her composure finally breaks.

> **The Nightingale:** "They've started looking for the Bell again..."

She explains that the murdered nobleman was not necessarily the intended target. The medallion was the real object of interest, and several powerful figures in the theatre may have been searching for it. Before she can explain what the Bell represents, a masked assassin emerges from the theatre rafters and kills the last surviving witness. The assassin escapes across the rooftops into the rain.

#### 6) Identify the Real Conspiracy

The party pieces together the evidence:

- The victim was moved after his death.
- The dagger was planted to create a simple explanation.
- Several witnesses were pressured into repeating the same story.
- Émile saw someone moving through the rafters.
- The murdered nobleman carried a fragment of the bell medallion.
- The invitation and medallion bear the same symbol.
- A masked assassin was searching for the same object.

The party learns that the murder was connected to the recovery of the medallion rather than a simple political assassination. The Nightingale confirms that the Bell is connected to a secret she has been investigating for years, but refuses to explain its full meaning.

> **The Nightingale:** "The murder was never meant to silence one nobleman. It was meant to bury a secret."

The party has cleared enough of their immediate suspicion to continue investigating, but the real conspiracy remains unresolved.

As the investigation concludes, **A Song for the Guilty** is completed and the follow-up quest [[Quests/Suereé Quests/The Silence Between Notes\|The Silence Between Notes]] begins.

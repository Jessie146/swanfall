#### Description

The rain never truly stops in [[Locations/Primary Locations/Suerré\|Suerré]]. It gathers in the forgotten canals beneath the city, seeps through the foundations of abandoned jazz clubs, and turns the old wine cellars into flooded passageways. By the time the party receives word from [[Characters/Suerre Characters/The Nightingale\|The Nightingale]], the murdered noble’s final informant has already disappeared.

[[Characters/Suerre Characters/The Nightingale\|The Nightingale]] believes the city guard is closing in, and whatever the informant knew may soon be lost. Following a trail through the abandoned clubs, flooded cellars, and forgotten canals, the party finds him dead, but his violin still holds one final secret. Hidden inside the instrument is a piece of coded sheet music. The notes are not music at all. They form a map, pointing beneath Suerré.

#### Expanded Objectives

#### 1) Locate the Noble's Final Informant

Following the events of [[Quests/Suereé Quests/A Song for the Guilty\|A Song for the Guilty]], [[Characters/Suerre Characters/The Nightingale\|The Nightingale]] asks the party to locate the murdered noble’s final informant before the city guard reaches him.

> **The Nightingale:** “The guard will search the obvious places first. We cannot allow them to reach him before we do.”
> **The Nightingale:** “Find him. Question him if he is still alive. If he is not, find out what he died protecting.”

The informant was last seen near the abandoned jazz clubs in the old entertainment district of [[Locations/Primary Locations/Suerré\|Suerré]].

#### 2) Search the Abandoned Jazz Clubs

The party searches the deserted clubs, where collapsed stages, ruined bars, and warped records remain beneath layers of dust.

> **[CUT OF YOUR JIB]** "Wait, what are those?" **(DC 10)**

A successful [[Attributes/Cut Of Your Jib (CTJ)\|Cut Of Your Jib (CTJ)]] check reveals muddy footprints leading behind one of the stages. A concealed entrance leads into the old service tunnels beneath the district.

> **[CERTAIN JE SAIS QUOI]** "Who was here before us?" **(DC 10)**

A successful [[Attributes/Certain Je Ne Sais Quoi (JNSQ)\|Certain Je Ne Sais Quoi (JNSQ)]] check reveals that the clubs were once used as meeting places by musicians, merchants, and informants. The murdered noble may have used them to exchange information.

The party discovers a torn piece of paper containing a partial message:

> “...beneath the cellar. Follow the water until the bells stop.”

#### 3) Explore the Flooded Wine Cellar

The tunnels lead into a series of flooded wine cellars beneath abandoned merchant houses. Broken barrels float in the dark water, while faded delivery symbols cover the walls. The party may cross the cellars along the remaining walkways, wade through the water, or search for a higher route through the storage rooms.

>- The Party: “These labels all bear the same symbol. I've seen it before.”
>- The Party: [CHILDLIKE WONDER] “The markings on these bottles aren't decorative. They're connected to the invitation.” (DC 12)

A successful [[Attributes/Childlike Wonder (CHI)\|Childlike Wonder (CHI)]] check identifies several wine labels bearing the same symbol found on the noble’s invitation. The labels are too damaged to identify, but the party can continue searching the cellars.

>- The Party: “That gate is the only way forward."
>- The Party: [GUMPTION] “Stand back. I'll get it open.” (DC 12)

A successful [[Attributes/Gumption (GMP)\|Gumption (GMP)]] check allows the party to force open a rusted gate and reach the lower tunnels without becoming trapped by the rising water. The gate refuses to budge. The party must find another route through the cellars or risk being caught by the rising water.

In the deepest cellar, the party finds a discarded violin case. It is empty, but the velvet lining has been recently disturbed.

#### 4) Follow the Forgotten Canals

The trail continues into the forgotten canals beneath [[Locations/Primary Locations/Suerré\|Suerré]]. These waterways run beneath the oldest parts of the city and are now used by smugglers, fugitives, and those who wish to move unseen. The canals are partially flooded, forcing the party to navigate narrow ledges and submerged passages.

>- The Party: “Someone has used this mooring recently.”
>- The Party: [CUT OF YOUR JIB] “The water hasn't washed these marks away yet. Someone came through here recently.” (DC 12)

A successful [[Attributes/Cut Of Your Jib (CTJ)\|Cut Of Your Jib (CTJ)]] check reveals a small boat recently tied to a broken mooring post. The party notices the broken mooring post but cannot determine how recently it was used.

>- The Party: “You there. Have you seen anyone come through here?”
>- The Party: [MOXIE] “We're looking for someone who may be in danger. If you saw anything, now is the time to tell us.” (DC 13)

A successful [[Attributes/Moxie (MOX)\|Moxie (MOX)]] check allows the party to question a canal scavenger, who claims to have seen a man carrying a violin being followed by two masked figures.

> **Canal Scavenger:** “He was in a hurry. Kept looking behind him. Then the masked ones came."
> **Canal Scavenger:** “I heard shouting from the tunnel. After that, nothing.”

The scavenger points towards a collapsed tunnel. If failure, the scavenger refuses to get involved, but his nervous glances suggest that he knows more than he is willing to say.

#### 5) Find the Informant

The party finds the informant in a chamber beyond the collapsed tunnel. He is dead, seated against the wall with his violin beside him. There are no obvious signs of a struggle. His pockets have been emptied, and his throat bears a narrow wound. The city guard has not yet arrived.

>- The Party: “Someone searched this body.”
>- The Party: [CERTAIN JE NE SAIS QUOI] “The wound is one thing. But whoever searched him knew exactly what they were looking for.” (DC 14)

A successful [[Attributes/Certain Je Ne Sais Quoi (JNSQ)\|Certain Je Ne Sais Quoi (JNSQ)]] check reveals that the body was searched after death. Whoever killed him was looking for something specific. If failure, yhe party can tell the informant was searched, but cannot determine what the killer was looking for.

>- The Party: “The violin is still here. Why would they leave it behind?”
>- The Party: [CHILDLIKE WONDER] “The case is ruined, but the instrument isn't. Someone took care to keep it dry.” (DC 12)

A successful [[Attributes/Childlike Wonder (CHI)\|Childlike Wonder (CHI)]] check reveals that the violin is unusually well maintained despite the condition of the case. The instrument appears to have been protected from the water. If failure, the violin appears damaged but intact. The party cannot tell whether it was deliberately protected or simply survived the flooding.

Searching the body reveals a small brass key and a fragment of black wax bearing the same mark associated with the conspiracy.

#### 6) Recover the Sheet Music

The brass key opens a hidden compartment inside the violin. Within it is a folded sheet of music covered in unfamiliar markings. At first, the page appears to be a badly written musical composition. The notes are inconsistent, the measures do not follow any recognizable structure, and several symbols have been repeated where no musical pattern would require them.

>- The Party: “This doesn't look like music.”
>- The Party: [CHILDLIKE WONDER] “The notes repeat in groups. There must be a pattern.” (DC 12)

A successful [[Attributes/Childlike Wonder (CHI)\|Childlike Wonder (CHI)]] check reveals that the notes are arranged according to a deliberate sequence. The repeated phrases and unusual pauses suggest that the sheet is concealing something rather than describing a melody. If failure, the party cannot identify a clear pattern, but the unusual arrangement suggests that the sheet may be a coded message.

#### 6.5 Puzzle: The Song Beneath the City

The sheet music contains five short musical phrases, each ending on a different note. The notes themselves are meaningless, but the rests between them form a pattern. When the party plays the correct notes, or identifies the correct pauses, the sheet music reveals a series of symbols:

> A bell. A wine barrel. A canal gate. A downward stair.

These correspond to landmarks encountered during the quest:

>- Bell - The abandoned jazz clubs
>- Wine barrel - The flooded wine cellars
>- Canal gate - The forgotten canals
>- Downward stair - The hidden entrance beneath Suerré

The party must arrange the landmarks in the order they were encountered. When placed correctly, the symbols form a route rather than a sentence.

>[!Quote |mark] [[Characters/Suerre Characters/The Nightingale\|The Nightingale]]
>“You were listening for a melody. The informant was leaving directions.”

The party can approach the puzzle in several ways:

>- [CHILDLIKE WONDER] — Recognise that the notes repeat in groups rather than forming a melody. (DC 12)
>- [CERTAIN JE NE SAIS QUOI] — Notice that the rests correspond to the pauses between the landmarks on the journey. (DC 14)
>- [INTELLIGENCE] — Compare the symbols with the locations already visited and arrange them correctly. (DC 15)
>- Ask [[Characters/Suerre Characters/The Nightingale\|The Nightingale]] to explain the significance of the symbols.

A failed check does not prevent progress. Instead, the party may take the sheet music to [[Characters/Suerre Characters/Theodore Marceau\|Theodore Marceau]] at [[Locations/Secondary Locations/Suereé Locations/The House of the Silent Note\|The House of the Silent Note]] in [[Locations/Primary Locations/Suerré\|Suerré]], who recognises the pattern and explains that the rests are the important part.

> Theodore Marceau: “That isn't a song. No musician would write a melody with pauses like these.”
> Theodore Marceau: “The notes are only the cover. The silence between them is what matters.”

> The Party: “Can you read it?”

> Theodore Marceau: “Not as music. But I know these markings. They belong to the old city.”
> Theodore Marceau: “Whoever wrote this wasn't trying to be heard. They were trying to be followed.”

The music isn't music. It's a map.

#### 7) Discover Where the Map Leads

Once solved, the final sequence changes from four landmarks into a fifth symbol: a downward-pointing crown.

The Nightingale recognises it immediately.

> The Nightingale: “That isn't a street. It isn't a canal.”
> The Nightingale: “It's the old royal seal. Whatever it was built to protect, the city has spent centuries pretending it never existed.”

The map points to a sealed passage beneath Suerré, beginning the next quest: [[Quests/Suereé Quests/Masks Beneath Masks\|Masks Beneath Masks]]

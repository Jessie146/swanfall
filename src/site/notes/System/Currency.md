Reputation medallions

Root: er- (from Protolang /jer/, "piece"). Different dialects used a different initial vowel or diphthong (e.g. /ae/,/i/,/ei/) but eventually converged on middle ground /e/. Denizens still can't agree on the exact realisation of /r/, but no one ever really does - so, while a fun way to figure out where someone may be from, it remains intelligible cross-dialectally
Different denominations 
- Era (/er/+a - pronounceable and useable by near anyone, low vowel for the lowest value, fronted for a straightforward, but not always gained upfront, piece of currency) 
- Ere (/er/+e - minimal extra effort required for the vowel's production and gaining this currency; middle vowel for a commonly encountered unit, still fronted and relatively openly available) 
- Eri (/er/+i - the tongue strains to reach this high vowel, locking the face in a fake smile as often seen in the echelons where this unit is commonplace; it is still a fronted vowel - just as this denomination seems high to most, but is actually middling to those who see beyond the surface) 
- Ero (/er/+o - the vowel does not sit as highly, but the tongue draws back to pronounce it - the unit's availability is obscured from most; The vowel's perfect roundness reflects the surprise and admiration those using this denomination tend to attract) 
- Eru (/er/+u - the tongue stays hidden back, but stretches higher - a unit whose value is usually reserved for the quietly, obscenely rich; round, but tighter than the /o/, the vowel has a tone both ominous and awestruck)
